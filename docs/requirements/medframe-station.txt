Extend Medframe with “Medframe Station”: a unified device workspace that reduces navigation and lets clinics continue work across desktop computers, clinician tablets, patient kiosks and TV displays.

The objective is one connected patient visit across devices, with focused interfaces for each user. Reuse the existing Django clinical, appointment, queue, billing and authentication systems.

1. DEVICE SETUP AND PAIRING

Add a Devices & Stations area for authorized clinic staff.

Staff can create and manage:
- Clinician tablets
- Patient kiosks
- Queue displays

Each device has a name, clinic, branch, mode, permitted functions, connection status and revocation control.

An authorized staff member generates a short-lived, single-use pairing code or QR code. The receiving device opens a public pairing page, enters or scans the code, and receives its assigned mode after approval.

The pairing page itself must expose no patient information.

Pairing identifies and configures the device. Clinical access must still require a named, authenticated staff identity and existing clinical permissions. Patient kiosks and TV displays must never inherit staff permissions.

Use server-enforced expiry, attempt limits, secure sessions and immediate revocation. Never use a permanent shared code as unrestricted access to the clinic.

2. CLINICIAN TABLET: ONE CONSULTATION WORKSPACE

Create a touchscreen-friendly, full-screen workspace inspired by the existing dental editor.

Keep the active patient and visit visible, with:
- Patient identity and visit status
- Relevant allergies and medical alerts
- Dental chart and individual tooth/surface selection
- Anatomical chart where applicable
- Point annotations and clinical notes
- SOAP notes, diagnoses, procedures and treatment plan
- Clinical documents and images
- Queue/service status
- Bill summary and authorized payment actions
- Save, finish consultation, lock and handoff controls

Use a central chart/editor with contextual panels and accessible tabs or drawers. Keep essential actions visible without navigating through several application pages.

Differentiate draft, saving, saved, failed and finalized states clearly. Preserve entered work during connection failures.

The tablet must edit the existing consultation draft using its existing version/conflict controls. It must not create a separate consultation or competing clinical data store.

Finalized records remain immutable; corrections use attributed amendments.

3. CONTINUE ON ANOTHER DEVICE / HAND OVER RESPONSIBILITY

From the desktop or tablet, provide “Continue on another device”.

Before transfer:
- Save and acknowledge the current draft.
- Clearly identify any unsaved changes.
- Generate a temporary handoff code or QR code.

The receiving authorized device opens the same patient, visit, chart selection and saved draft.

Use one active editing device by default. After an accepted transfer, the previous device becomes read-only for that consultation. Expired or abandoned transfers leave ownership clear.

Also support handing responsibility to another authorized clinician:
- Identify the receiving clinician.
- Show a concise handoff summary and outstanding tasks.
- Require acceptance.
- Record who handed over, who accepted and when.
- Apply any responsible-provider changes through existing authorization rules.

Device transfer must not silently change clinical responsibility, finalize care or confirm payment.

4. PATIENT KIOSK HOME

Provide four large actions:
- Book an appointment
- Check in for an existing appointment
- Join the walk-in queue
- Pay for clinic services

Use readable text, large touch targets, minimal typing and clear progress.

Include Back, Home, Cancel and staff assistance.

After completion, cancellation or inactivity, clear patient-specific state and return home. Warn before timeout and allow an accessible extension.

Never expose the previous patient’s information through Back navigation, cached screens or persistent form state.

5. PATIENT IDENTIFICATION, BOOKING AND CHECK-IN

Reuse supported patient verification methods. A device-pairing code is not patient verification.

Do not reveal existing records or balances based only on a matching name or date of birth.

Support minimal new-patient registration. Route uncertain identity matches to staff review rather than automatically linking records or creating duplicates.

Booking:
- Choose service.
- Choose doctor when applicable.
- Select an available date and time.
- Review a summary before confirming.
- Prevent duplicate submissions and overlapping reservations in the backend.

Check-in:
- Verify the appointment and permitted arrival window.
- Confirm arrival.
- Admit the patient to the appropriate same-day queue according to clinic rules.
- Issue the queue number and next instruction.

Clearly distinguish a future appointment confirmation from same-day queue admission.

6. WALK-INS AND DEPARTMENT QUEUES

Support consultation, laboratory, cashier and other configured departments.

Maintain one visit with linked department queue stages. Transfers must preserve history without creating duplicate patient visits or duplicate clinical encounters.

Extend the current provider-linked queue model where necessary; cashier and laboratory queues must not require a fictitious doctor.

Authorized staff can call, recall, skip, prioritize, transfer, cancel and complete tickets. Record reasons and actors where appropriate.

Issue a unique ticket showing:
- Queue number
- Department
- Issue time
- Next step

Support on-screen tickets and optional printing when a printer is configured. A printer failure must not create a second ticket.

Show waiting estimates only when reliable data exists. Otherwise show queue position or an honest unavailable state.

7. PAYMENTS

After patient verification, show the selected patient’s outstanding bills, service descriptions, existing payments and current balance.

Support:
- Digital payment through a configured payment provider
- Cash payment at an authorized cashier
- Kiosk cash acceptance only with compatible configured hardware

Treat digital payment integration as a new integration where it is not already implemented. Do not simulate a successful charge or label an unconfigured provider as available.

Verify payment through trusted backend/provider confirmation before recording success. A browser redirect, screenshot or patient button press is not payment confirmation.

Handle pending, failed, cancelled and successful payments. Use idempotency and reconciliation so retries do not create duplicate charges or ledger entries.

Generate a receipt after confirmed payment.

Clinician tablets may display payment status. Recording cash or other manual financial actions requires the appropriate billing permission; being a doctor does not automatically grant it.

Make payment-before-service and payment-after-service configurable by service or department, with explicit, audited staff exceptions.

8. TV QUEUE DISPLAY

Create a dedicated read-only display with no staff navigation.

Show:
- Currently called queue numbers
- Assigned doctor, room or counter
- Upcoming queue numbers
- Clinic announcements
- Current clinic time

Do not show patient names, contact information, diagnoses, balances or internal record identifiers.

Use a dedicated minimal public-display response rather than reusing staff record serializers.

Update automatically after queue changes. Support reconnection, a visible stale/offline indication, optional sound and spoken queue-number announcements.

Provide an “Enable announcements” control and prevent duplicate announcements after reconnection.

9. DEVICE AND CONNECTION BEHAVIOR

Make clinician tablets, shared kiosks and displays independently lockable and revocable.

Keep staff identity, patient identity, device identity and visit ownership distinct.

During disconnection:
- Do not claim an unsaved draft is saved.
- Do not confirm an appointment or payment locally.
- Preserve recoverable clinician input appropriately.
- Tell patients when staff assistance is required.
- Mark TV information as stale.

Provide a browser-based focused mode first. Describe any operating-system kiosk lockdown or printer/payment hardware setup as separate configuration requirements.

10. IMPLEMENTATION AND DELIVERY

First inspect the current implementation and produce a written design and implementation plan identifying reused features, required extensions and unresolved provider/hardware dependencies.

Use Graphify for impact tracing, Jev for sanitized architectural judgments and Impeccable for interface design.

Implement natively without subagents. Preserve existing data, accounts, MFA, clinical authorization and rollback checkpoints.

Use actual shared shadcn components, Tailwind, readable focused modules, Prettier and Ruff.

Deliver connected features per phase. Prioritize device pairing plus clinician handoff, then patient self-service and department queues, then payment integration and TV display.

Use synthetic data and one accumulated, proportional verification pass per phase. Log unrelated findings; repair necessary activation blockers with focused confirmation. Activate completed, approved phases locally.

Report actual functionality, remaining gaps and external configuration requirements honestly.
